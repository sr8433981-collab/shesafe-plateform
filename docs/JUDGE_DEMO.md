# Judge Demo — click by click

**Total: 3 minutes 50 seconds (230s).** Ten steps, deterministic, served by
`GET /api/demo/script` and rendered on the dashboard as *Judge walkthrough*, so
the script you are reading and the script in the product cannot disagree. Each
step can also scroll to the exact block it is about, not just to a screen.

Every simulated surface is badged `SIMULATED` in the interface. Nothing in this
demo is a mock-up of a feature that does not exist.

```
PREVENT → ASSESS → NAVIGATE SAFELY → PROTECT JOURNEY → ACTIVATE SOS →
SHARE LOCATION → COORDINATE TRUSTED PEOPLE → DOCUMENT INCIDENT
```

---

## Before the demo (60 seconds, not part of the 4 minutes)

```bash
python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r backend/requirements.txt
SHESAFE_DEMO_MODE=1 .venv/bin/python3 backend/wsgi.py
# → http://127.0.0.1:5000
```

Pre-flight:

1. Open **two** browser windows side by side. Window A stays on the app.
   Window B is reserved for the guardian link in step 8.
2. **Leave location off at first.** Step 2 turns it on, so the consent gate is
   part of the demo rather than something you forgot to set up.
3. Microphone permission is **not** needed. Do not enable Voice SOS.
4. Confirm the DEMO ribbon is visible at the top. If it is not, Demo Mode is off
   and the simulated surfaces will not be labelled.
5. Confirm the network. Overpass and OSRM are volunteer-run public services; the
   failure drill at the bottom of this file covers what to say if they are down.
6. Optionally: DevTools → Application → Service Worker, confirm `sw.js` is
   activated. That is the offline story.

Demo credentials: `demo@shesafe.local` / `shesafe-demo`. The sign-in screen has a
**Sign in as the demo account** button, so you never type during the demo.

To reset between rehearsals, restart the server or `POST /api/demo/reset`.
Reset closes any open incident and journey and revokes every share link. It
**keeps** past incidents, so the record you showed a judge is still there.

## The walkthrough

### Step 1 · Dashboard — 20s

**Do:** Load the app. Click **Sign in as the demo account**.

**Say:** "A signed-out visitor sees this screen and nothing else — the
authenticated views cannot be reached by typing a hash. The session is a signed
`HttpOnly` cookie, and CSRF is enforced on every write."

**Then read the dashboard top to bottom without clicking.** "One control
dominates. Below it, the safety status. Then Journey Guard, live location,
trusted contacts, safe places, safety intelligence — in that order, because that
is the order you need them in. Nothing else is on this screen."

**Point at** the *"If you need emergency services"* block. "112 opens your
phone's dialler and you press call. SheSafe cannot place that call, and we will
not pretend otherwise."

---

### Step 2 · Safety score — 25s

**Do:** Click **Enable location** and accept the browser prompt. Then click
**Why this score?**

**Say:** "Safety score out of 100 — not risk, because risk is a word nobody
outside this room can act on. The band, the confidence and the data coverage are
printed next to it, because a number without its coverage is a marketing claim."

**Point at the coverage line.** "If the coverage is thin, it says so on the same
screen as the number."

**If asked about accuracy:** "We do not know and we do not claim. This is a
transparent weighted rule set with no trained model, so no accuracy figure is
printed anywhere in this product."

---

### Step 3 · Why this score? — 30s

**Do:** Scroll to **Why this score?**, then to **What's affecting my safety?**

**Say:** "One paragraph, generated by the engine from the same six values that
produced the score, so the words cannot disagree with the arithmetic."

**Then the ledger.** "Two columns: what pushed risk up, what pushed it down.
Each line is a factor's movement away from the neutral prior the engine assumes
when it has no data, with the sign shown."

**Then the factor list.** "Each factor shows its points — what it actually
contributes to the score — and its baseline, and the movement is the difference
between the two. Add the *points* column up and you land on the risk score."

**Point at** the full factor list. "Every factor shows its weight, its points,
its baseline, its reason, and where the data came from — observed or not
observed. A factor with no prior is listed as unchanged rather than being given
a fake positive or negative sign."

---

### Step 4 · A safer route — 25s

**Do:** Click **Safer routes**. Press **Use my position** for the origin, type
the destination, press **Find routes**.

```
From   28.63280, 77.21970      (Connaught Place)
To     28.65010, 77.24120      (Lajpat Nagar)
```

**Say:** "Real OSRM road geometry, three alternatives, each safety-scored at nine
points along the corridor rather than just the midpoint."

**Point at the per-option line.** "'Adds six minutes and scores fourteen points
safer than the fastest option.' The whole trade-off in one sentence."

**Then point at the compare header.** "If the data is too thin to separate the
options, it says *Limited safety data available* instead of inventing a
difference. That state exists and it is not hidden."

**If OSRM is slow or down:** "Public volunteer infrastructure. The screen says so
honestly instead of hanging — that is the fallback we built."

---

### Step 5 · Journey Guard — 20s

**Do:** Click **Journey**. Set *Expected travel time* to **2 minutes**. Pick a
trusted contact. Click **Start Journey Guard**.

**Say:** "Tell it where you are going and how long you expect to take, and
attach the contact who would be told."

**Point at the ladder.** "The escalation ladder is shown in full, in order.
Nobody is ever alerted without an explicit step."

**Point at the limitation card.** "Escalation only advances while this page is
open. Browsers cannot reliably wake a closed tab, so we say that here, in the
active state, not in a footnote."

---

### Step 6 · What changed? — 25s

**Do:** Scroll to **What changed?** and press **Time of day**.

**Say:** "Change one input, and the same engine re-scores and subtracts. Before,
the factor that moved, after. Not a second model — the same weights, run twice."

**Point at the middle block.** "That is the factor, and the number under it is
its movement in risk points. The explanation below is assembled from the two
assessments."

**Then press **Safety data**, then **Route**, then **Location**, quickly.**

**Say:** "Four inputs can legitimately change a score, and the endpoint refuses
any other — so the label on screen always matches the arithmetic. If you ask for
a comparison that changed nothing, it is refused rather than reported as
'unchanged'."

**The honest branch — say this out loud if coverage is thin:** "It says *Limited
safety data available*. It will not tell me which factor caused that move,
because it does not know. That refusal is the feature."

---

### Step 7 · SOS — 30s

**Do:** On the dashboard, **press and hold the SOS orb** for one second. Release
during the countdown — do **not** let it run out yet.

**Say:** "Press and hold, because a pocket should not be able to alert anybody.
Keyboard and assistive activation work too, and there is a labelled one-tap
alternative for anyone who cannot hold."

**During the countdown:** "Ten seconds to cancel, because a false alarm must cost
nothing. It says exactly when it will activate, and cancelling is one tap.
Nothing has been sent to anyone yet — and it says that."

**Now let it run out.** Watch `Activating` — do not skip past it.

**Say:** "It does not say *SOS ACTIVE* yet, because the incident does not exist
until the server has written it. That is the difference between a real system and
a demo that animates."

**Then point at the console, top to bottom:** "The two actions are first. Then
six facts: position, GPS accuracy, how old that fix is, whether sharing is
active, what happened to the contacts, and what SheSafe can and cannot reach.
**Emergency services: UNAVAILABLE** — read from the server, not hardcoded."

---

### Step 8 · Guardian view — 25s

**Do:** Click **Live location**. Click **Start live sharing**. Click **Copy
link**. Paste into **window B**.

**Say:** "256-bit random token, only a SHA-256 hash stored at rest, so a database
leak cannot be replayed. It expires automatically. No user id in the URL — a
guessed `?user=` does nothing."

**In window B:** "One word at the top says whether you can trust what you are
looking at: **LIVE**, **STALE**, **RECONNECTING** or **REVOKED**. Under it, that
word explained in a sentence. Then current status, current location, last
updated, incident time, sharing state, and the timeline."

**Do this to prove it:** back in window A, click **Revoke**. Watch window B.

**Say:** "Revoked. Instant. The link is dead and the guardian view says
**REVOKED** plainly instead of showing a stale dot."

**Optional:** re-start sharing so you can continue with two windows open.

---

### Step 9 · The incident record — 15s

**Do:** Click **Incident history**. Click **Show the full timeline**.

**Say:** "Every event with its timestamp, its type, its status and a sentence
explaining it — armed, cancel window, activated, tracking link created, position
captured, each contact attempt, stood down."

**Point at the counters.** "Delivered, simulated, unavailable. With no messaging
provider configured, every attempt is recorded as **SIMULATED** and labelled as
such. We do not merge those into a single green tick."

---

### Step 10 · Stand down — 15s

**Do:** Click **Dashboard**. Click **I am safe — stand down**. Confirm.

**Say:** "One confirmation, because standing down is a real action. The record
keeps everything, and the stand-down goes out on the same channels, recorded with
the same honest status."

**Close on this, unprompted:**

> "One thing to be clear about before you ask: SheSafe does not contact police,
> fire or ambulance, and it never will pretend to. Every screen says so, the
> capability manifest reads `UNAVAILABLE` for police dispatch because that is
> what the server reports, and Call 112 is a button that opens your dialler. That
> is the whole design position: show the truth, including where the truth is a
> limitation."

**If time allows (30s), open *Privacy & capability*.** "Retention policy, data
export, account deletion, and the capability manifest read live from the server."

## Anticipated questions

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| "Is it connected to 112?" | No, and we do not claim it is. No Indian emergency service offers a public API. The button dials on your device. |
| "How accurate is the safety score?" | We do not know and we do not claim. Transparent weighted rules, no training data. Here is the arithmetic — the app shows it too. |
| "How do you know what changed the score?" | By running the same engine twice and subtracting. No second model, no narrative written by hand. If the data is too thin it refuses to name a cause. |
| "Does it work without internet?" | The dashboard, the SOS control and the emergency numbers work; the service worker caches an offline page with every published helpline. Recording an incident and alerting a contact need the server, and the screen says so rather than pretending. |
| "What is your stack?" | Flask application factory, SQLite with WAL and additive migrations, zero runtime dependencies beyond Flask. Vanilla ES modules and Leaflet on the front, no build step. |
| "Why no React?" | A build step is a failure mode at demo time. The hash router, the module graph and a shared UI kit cover what we needed without one. |
| "How is this different?" | It does not lie. Most demos of this genre show a fake dispatch screen. This one shows the delivery status of each alert, shows you the movement in a score and the factor responsible for it, and prints a confidence figure even when it is embarrassing. |
| "Who can moderate community reports?" | A `moderator` role, enforced on the server. Demo Mode opens it to any account so the flow can be shown, and the API says which permission model applied. |
| "What happens to my data?" | Retention per category, a JSON export of everything, and account deletion that revokes the session immediately and removes names, numbers and coordinates. Incident records are kept without identifiers, and we say so rather than quietly rewriting them. |
| "Is it accessible?" | Machine-checked, not human-audited. Focus restoration, contrast computed in both colour schemes, 44px targets, ARIA, reduced motion — all asserted by the suite. Nobody has tested it with a screen reader and a real user, so we claim no certification. |

## Failure drills — what to do if something breaks mid-demo

| Symptom | What it actually means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No route cards in step 4 | OSRM or Overpass is unreachable (public volunteer services) | Say so, move on. The screen states the failure honestly — that is the point. |
| "Limited safety data available" in step 6 | Overpass did not respond, so coverage is thin | **Do not try to fix it.** Say: "It is refusing to name a cause because it does not have enough data. That refusal is the feature." |
| Guardian shows RECONNECTING | A transient network failure | Leave it four seconds. It recovers with backoff and keeps the last known position visible with its age. That is the behaviour, not a bug. |
| Guardian shows REVOKED | You already revoked it in a previous run | Re-start sharing in step 8 and use the new link. |
| Contacts list is empty | The demo seed did not run | Add one contact; it takes fifteen seconds and the verification flow is worth showing. |
| Score is identical to last time | It is deterministic. That is a feature. | Say so: "Same inputs, same score. That is what makes it auditable." |
| A location chip is missing | The places provider failed | The dashboard says "Enable location to find real police stations…" rather than inventing a list. That is correct. |
